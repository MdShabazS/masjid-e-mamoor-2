**Reviewed:** 15 October 2026 (India time)  
**Scope:** Desk research and read-only repository inspection. No hosted account, live database, WhatsApp template, Google Drive folder, APK device upgrade, physical geofence, legal opinion, or restore drill was tested. Existing working-tree modifications were left intact.

## Research conclusion

Task 2's research question is answered: the proposed Android/web/Supabase architecture is feasible in principle, but a free tier and a 4 TB Drive allocation alone do not meet all backup, uptime and WhatsApp requirements. The highest-risk design decisions are financial edit/delete, public receipt-number lookup, broadly visible salary/performance data, password-only high-privilege accounts, and location-based attendance. None is a claim of production readiness.

Sources were checked against current official WhatsApp Business, Google Drive API, Supabase, Android, W3C, PostgreSQL, OWASP, and MeitY publications. WhatsApp's official FAQ states per-message billing with the first 1,000 service messages per business phone number each month free after 1 October 2026; an older paragraph on the same page and another Meta pricing page still describe prior pricing. Use the newer dated statement and verify the live business rate card. Developer Facebook documentation returned HTTP 429 during this pass, so its linked webhook and pricing pages need rechecking during actual integration. Exact solicitation template classification cannot be known until Meta approves the proposed content.

## Source conclusions that drive architecture

1. [WhatsApp policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/) requires recipient opt-in, approved business-initiated templates and honoring opt-out. [Official FAQ](https://whatsappbusiness.com/resources/faq/) gives category/market-dependent billing and the current service-message threshold. Avoid pricing promises before account/template approval.
2. [Supabase backup documentation](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/backups) recommends independent exports for Free and says database backups exclude Storage objects. Therefore backup scope must include both database and file bytes, with restoration evidence.
3. [Google Drive API limits](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/guides/limits) impose request quotas, retry behavior, 1 TB/day project egress threshold and a 750 GB/day upload condition explicitly for Workspace users. Do not apply that latter condition blindly to a personal Google One account. Standard API use has no additional charge at publication, while Google flags potential future charges for excess quota usage.
4. [Android signing](https://developer.android.com/studio/publish/app-signing), [Android location](https://developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/location/permissions) and [geofencing](https://developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/location/geofencing) require release-key custody and realistic location fallback; the 100 m threshold is at the edge of typical accuracy, not secure proof of presence. [W3C Geolocation](https://www.w3.org/TR/geolocation/) requires a secure context and user permission on web.
5. [PostgreSQL transaction isolation](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/transaction-iso.html) and [Supabase RLS](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/postgres/row-level-security) support atomic posting/row-level restrictions; application implementation must still handle idempotency, retries, direct URL/RPC access and role migration. [OWASP file upload](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/File_Upload_Cheat_Sheet.html) informs bill/memo/image controls.
6. [MeitY's 2025 Rules](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf) and [Act commencement notification](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/c56ceae6c383460ca69577428d36828b.pdf) specify phased commencement. Implement privacy design now and obtain qualified local review before deployment; this document is engineering research, not legal advice.
